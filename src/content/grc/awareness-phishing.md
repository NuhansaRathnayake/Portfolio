## Aim

After the session, every person can spot a suspicious message and knows exactly what to do next.

## Outline

1. **Why it matters (5 minutes).** One real-style example of how a single clicked link can lead to a compromised account.
2. **Five warning signs (10 minutes).** Unexpected urgency, an unfamiliar sender, a mismatched link address, a request for credentials or payment, and an attachment you were not expecting.
3. **Try it (10 minutes).** Staff review six sample emails in pairs and decide which are genuine.
4. **What to do (5 minutes).** Do not click. Report it using the report button or by forwarding it to the office manager. Reporting is always welcome, even if the message turns out to be harmless.

## Handout

A one-page card with the five warning signs and the reporting steps, kept near each desk.

## Measuring success

Track the number of reports staff make and the number of simulated phishing messages that get clicked. A rise in reports is a good sign, not a bad one.
