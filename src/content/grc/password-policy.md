*Sample for a fictional organisation. Based on the approach in NIST SP 800-63B, which favours length and breach checks over forced rotation.*

## Rules

1. **Length over complexity.** Passwords are at least 14 characters. A passphrase of several unrelated words is encouraged.
2. **Unique everywhere.** No password is reused across accounts. Staff use the studio-approved password manager.
3. **Check against breaches.** Passwords that appear in known breach lists are rejected.
4. **Change only when needed.** Passwords are changed if there is evidence of compromise, not on a fixed calendar.
5. **Multi-factor authentication.** Required for email, file storage, the password manager and any admin account. An authenticator app is preferred to text messages.

## Why these rules

Forced regular changes push people towards predictable patterns, such as adding a number to the end. Length, uniqueness and a second factor give stronger protection with less effort for staff.

## Exceptions

Shared or service accounts need written approval from the director and a named owner.
