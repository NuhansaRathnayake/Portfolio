---
title: How project managers can embed risk management in IT projects
type: Article
summary: Four habits that turn the risk register from a document into part of how the project runs.
order: 8
---

A risk register that is written once and never opened is paperwork. A register that shapes the schedule, the budget and the weekly conversation is a project control. These four habits move it from one to the other.

## 1. Start in week one, with the whole team

Risks come from the people closest to the work. A short session in the first week, with the sponsor, the technical lead and the people who will use the system, finds more than a project manager can alone. Capture each risk as a cause, an event and a consequence, so it is specific enough to act on.

## 2. Score it, then decide what to do

Likelihood and impact on a simple 1 to 5 scale are enough. The score matters less than the decision it triggers: mitigate, transfer, accept or avoid. Every risk gets exactly one named owner.

## 3. Put the response in the plan

If the response to a risk is a task, it belongs in the schedule and the budget. A data migration risk becomes two trial migrations with dates. Otherwise the response exists only on paper.

## 4. Review it where decisions are made

Add the top five risks to the weekly status report. Mark what has changed, what has closed and what is new. When a risk happens, the register shows what was already planned, and when it does not happen, the team still learns whether the scoring was right.

## The link to GRC

Governance asks who is accountable, risk management asks what could go wrong, and compliance asks what must be shown. A project manager who keeps a living risk register, a clear RACI and a short evidence trail covers all three without extra bureaucracy.
