## Context

This is a self-initiated exercise for a fictional design studio with 12 people, no in-house IT staff and clients who send confidential brand assets. I wanted to practise the part of GRC that is hard: making frameworks useful to a small business that has never heard of them.

## My role

GRC analyst. I scoped the assessment, identified and scored the risks, mapped controls to two frameworks and wrote the roadmap.

## Problem and objectives

The studio's owner knew that client trust mattered but could not say where the biggest exposure was. The objectives were to:

- Identify the most likely and most damaging information risks.
- Show which controls were already in place and which were missing.
- Produce a short list of actions that fit a small team's time and budget.

## Approach

I started with risk rather than with the framework. Listing the risks first kept the work grounded in what could actually hurt the studio, then I used ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A as the control catalogue and NIST CSF 2.0 as the common language for the outcomes. I mapped a sample of seven controls rather than all 93, so the result stays readable.

## Deliverables

**Risk assessment.** Four risks, each scored on likelihood and impact, with a treatment and an owner.

**Control mapping and gap analysis.** Each ISO 27001 control is paired with the closest NIST CSF 2.0 outcome and rated as in place, partial or a gap.

<FrameworkMap />

**90-day roadmap.**

1. **Days 1 to 30:** turn on multi-factor authentication, move client files into the managed workspace and write a one-page information security policy.
2. **Days 31 to 60:** automate backups, test a restore, and introduce a joiner, mover and leaver checklist.
3. **Days 61 to 90:** run a 30-minute awareness session and draft a one-page incident response plan.

## Challenges and how I handled them

- **Too many controls for a small team.** I ranked actions by risk rating first and effort second, so the first 30 days address the three high risks.
- **Jargon.** The roadmap names things the owner recognises (client files, email accounts, backups) and keeps control numbers in the mapping table only.

## Outcome and lessons

The result is a 7-control mapping with 1 control in place, 2 partial and 4 gaps, plus a roadmap in three 30-day stages.

- Framework mapping is most valuable as a translation exercise: the same action can satisfy a control in both frameworks.
- A gap analysis only helps if it ends in an owner and a date, so every action in the roadmap has both.
- Next time I would validate the risk scores with the people who do the work, because likelihood is a judgement and a second opinion improves it.
